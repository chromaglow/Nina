The Wounded Healer: A Journey Through Pain, Healing, and TransformationThis piece was inspired by my dear friend, Andy LaPlegua of Combichrist and Icon of Coil.His prompt: "They tried to whitewash our pain, but blood leaves such a stubborn stain," from the song "Each Scar a Vision."Andy's willingness to create from pain rather than hide from it challenged me to ask a deeper question: What if our wounds are not only the source of creativity, but also the birthplace of healing? That question became the soul of this painting.At the center of the piece is a blue goddess, a symbol of the divine feminine, wisdom, and transformation. Within her is an open wound—a hole in her heart where pain has been exposed. Her heart bleeds not as a symbol of weakness, but of truth. What we suppress cannot heal. What we are willing to face can transform us.Living within her wounded heart is Chiron, the Wounded Healer from Greek mythology. Unlike the other centaurs, Chiron was wise, compassionate, and devoted his life to healing others. Yet after being struck by one of Hercules' poisoned arrows, he carried a wound that could never fully heal. Though he possessed the knowledge to heal others, he could not heal himself. Chiron's story reminds us that healing is not about erasing our wounds. Some experiences become part of us forever. We cannot change what happened, but we can transform our relationship with it. Through awareness, acceptance, and compassion, our deepest wounds become the source of our wisdom, empathy, creativity, and strength.For me, Chiron lives within the goddess's bleeding heart because I believe each of us carries a wounded healer within. The wound is not something to hide from or deny. It is something to meet with honesty. When we stop running from it, we begin to integrate it. The wound does not disappear—it becomes part of our story instead of the thing that controls us.This painting stopped being an artwork and became an initiation.Some paintings ask us to express our emotions.This one asked me to meet my pain.It demanded that I stop running from the wound in my own heart and finally sit with it. During its creation, I cried, screamed, collapsed onto the floor of my studio, and wept. I grieved not only heartbreak, but the pain I had carried for most of my life. It asked me to mourn what I had lost, what I never received, and the stories I had believed about myself.One idea echoed through every layer of paint:The greatest forms of suffering are the lies we tell ourselves.This painting asked me to confront those lies—the belief that I was unworthy, unchosen, broken, or somehow unlovable. It asked me to stop running from my shadow and instead welcome it with honesty and compassion. When we stop trying to erase our wounds and instead witness them with courage, they become something entirely different. The wound becomes the doorway. The scar becomes wisdom.When we numb our pain, we also numb our joy.
